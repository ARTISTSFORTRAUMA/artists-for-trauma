For more than 25 years, Eddie Donaldson has moved through graffiti and street art culture, not just as a writer, but as a builder, connecting artists with clients.

GuerillaOneA global network

What started on walls, railway cars and highway overpasses became something much larger.

GuerillaOne grew into a global network of artists, built organically through people, movement, and shared energy.

SixteenLouisville to Los Angeles

Born in Louisville, Kentucky, Eddie’s early life was structured and stable. That shifted at 16, when his mother passed away, and he had to move to Los Angeles to live with his aunt.

In a city defined by intensity and survival, he had to quickly learn how to make money, how to move, and how to take care of himself.

TCF & The Seventh LetterA brotherhood

In that environment, graffiti became an alternative path. Through crews like TCF, and The Seventh Letter, he not only found art, but a brotherhood that felt like home.

The beginningPainting for free

It began with painting for free; liquor stores, local spots, anywhere they could get wall space to display their art.

Eddie watched how the store owners reacted. They were excited. Proud to have one-of-a-kind artwork on their building. That reaction changed everything.

NegotiatingReal value

Eddie started negotiating. He asked for $20. Then $100. Then $200. Over time, what began as free work turned into real money, real value, and eventually large scale 6-figure commissions.

What looked like graffiti became something else. A way to build, to connect, and to create opportunity beyond the street and shape pop culture.

Before social mediaGuerillaOne.com

As the work expanded, so did the vision. Eddie created GuerillaOne, the first online graffiti art platform, at a time before social media, giving artists a way to see and share work from around the world.

“We needed to do this website where a guy in France can do a painting, and we can see what he did, and he can see what we did… and so we started GuerillaOne.com.”
Eddie Donaldson

Poster ©Shepard Fairey

What began as a name became a network, giving writers visibility, identity, and a way to communicate far beyond their immediate environment.

The GuerillaOne website created real opportunity. What lived on the street evolved into exhibitions, brand work, and paid projects that could support artists in a lasting way.

The culture moves mainstreamAuthentic & alive

In the early days, graffiti took real risk, stealing spray paint, painting illegally, constantly being on the lookout. But over time, it became more accessible as the culture moved mainstream.

With that came new possibilities, but also change. Some of the friction that shaped the work began to fade, requiring a new kind of adaptability to keep the culture authentic & alive.

Risk Rock StudiosA critical moment

A chance meeting at Risk Rock Studios brought Eddie into contact with Laura Sharpe, founder of Artists for Trauma, at a critical moment.

His youngest daughter was going through a crisis. Laura offered Eddie guidance, support, and real solutions during a deeply sensitive time.

That connection turned into action. Eddie recognized the healing power of art and became Laura’s collaborative partner.

“I know a lot about trauma, and I know a lot about art. So the idea that I can help heal myself by being of service to others, by connecting these dots, it’s twofold. It’s being of service to others and being of service to myself. It’s all encompassing to me.”
Eddie Donaldson

Mural ©Shepard Fairey

Louisville, KentuckyDirect impact

Through GuerillaOne and Artists for Trauma, Eddie and Laura bring artists into spaces where their work has a direct impact.

Working with local and international artists, they create meaningful, community-driven projects like the Muhammad Ali mural in Louisville, Kentucky, created by Shepard Fairey.

Where Ali grew upThe West End

The team chose the West End Louisville neighborhood for a reason. This is the socio-economic area where Muhammad Ali grew up.

“Art belongs where it can change the trajectory of life.”
Eddie Donaldson
“When you look at this mural, 25 feet from you, is a little tricycle on a patio where a family lives. We hope the child that rides that tricycle is inspired by the Ali mural and realizes that anything in life is possible.”
Eddie Donaldson
“As Eddie continues to evolve, he has stepped into the world of combat sports; building The Trust Agency.”

Combat sportsThe Trust Agency

TRUST is a marketing agency that represents athletes and promotions across mixed martial arts, boxing & wrestling.

It’s a new arena, but the approach is familiar, rooted in trust and creating opportunity.

LegacyWho he is

Eddie is proud of the relationships he continues to build along the way. At this point, the focus is simple: to be remembered not just for what he created, but for who he is.

“I’m 57 years old, and a majority of that time might not be what I want to be remembered for. So I’m trying to spend the rest of the time I have here on earth just being a good person.”
Eddie Donaldson
