AFT & Laura Sharpe's Artists Profile

Lilian Raven

Fashion Born From SurvivalDesigner · Seamstress · Sewing Teacher

Photo/Design Per BernalInterview Tattiahna WelshJune 2026

Inside Lilian’s studio, fashion feels less like an industry and more like an extension of who she is.

Every garment, fabric choice, and silhouette is approached with intention, craftsmanship, and bold creativity. She sees design as both construction and storytelling, using texture and shape to communicate identity.

As a Los Angeles-based fashion designer, seamstress, and sewing teacher, Lilian has remained committed to mentoring young creatives while helping preserve sewing as a craft passed from one generation to the next.

SurvivalA source of calm

Creativity entered Lilian’s life through survival. At four years old, she was severely burned by fire, leaving her with injuries that shaped much of her childhood and recovery.

During that period, one of the few things that brought her comfort was watching her mother sew. Seeing fabric transformed by hand became a source of calm during a time when very little else did.

Creativity became a way for Lilian to heal, rebuild her confidence, and rediscover herself beyond the trauma.

“I’m just a happy trauma survivor, thankful to still be alive and still be here in the world, and to be able to live my life to the fullest.”
Lilian Raven

13 years oldHer first sewing machine

At 13 years old, Lilian received her first sewing machine, finally allowing her to create for herself. What began as fascination quickly became something much deeper.

Lilian started making small pieces on her own, experimenting with fabric and teaching herself through the process. Sewing absorbed her in a way that nothing else had, giving her focus, purpose, and a creative outlet during a difficult chapter of her life.

What started inside her mother’s home slowly became the foundation for the life she would later build through fashion.

London to Los AngelesFrom Camden to Melrose

Lilian eventually opened her own storefront in Camden Town, London’s most recognizable spaces for avant-garde and underground fashion, before later bringing her work to Melrose Avenue in Los Angeles.

But as her career evolved, she realized her deepest connection to fashion was not being front-facing within the industry. What fulfilled her most was helping others discover confidence, healing, and self-expression through creativity.

That realization led her toward teaching and mentorship.

TeachingBringing the art back

Teaching sewing became just as meaningful to Lilian as designing clothing. As she worked with more young creatives, she saw how making something with your own hands could build confidence and self-expression.

“At the time, when I started, it was a dying art... so I just wanted to bring the art back into schools and communities.”

Stepping away from storefront fashion allowed Lilian to focus more deeply on mentorship, creativity, and connection through making.

Artists for TraumaOur core values aligned

Lilian’s connection with Artists for Trauma began through one of her sewing students, who introduced her to founder Laura Sharpe. What immediately resonated with Lilian was the organization’s shared understanding of survival, creativity, and emotional healing.

“Laura, when I spoke to her, her story impacted me because it’s all trauma-based... our core values aligned.”

Through Artists for Trauma, Lilian found another space where fashion and art could help people reconnect with themselves through creativity.

Turning Scars Into ArtFlipping the whole script

One of Lilian’s earliest collaborations with Artists for Trauma grew out of a project she had already been developing called “Turning Scars Into Art.”

“It was pictures of me artistically drawn, highlighting my scars and my features. It was done beautifully... it was just like flipping the whole script.”

Around the same time, Laura Sharpe was organizing an installation at California Rehabilitation Institute centered around trauma survivors and healing through art. Lilian’s work became part of the installation.

Love Your BodySpace to be seen

Another collaboration between Lilian, Laura, and Artists for Trauma came through Karen Michelle’s Love Your Body event. Representing Artists for Trauma, Lilian presented designs on a runway that challenged the fashion industry’s narrow standards of beauty and belonging.

Centered on empowerment and inclusivity, the event created space for women and girls of all shapes, sizes, ages, ethnicities, and abilities to feel seen and celebrated.

For Lilian, it reflected a belief that has guided her work from the beginning: fashion should create space for people to be authentically themselves.

FamilyThe next generation

Outside of fashion, one of Lilian’s proudest roles is being a mother. Creativity has always been part of her household, and she has encouraged the next generation to fully embrace who they are.

Over time, her son went from asking her to make clothes to learning how to create them himself alongside her in the studio, continuing the same creative connection Lilian once experienced beside her own mother.

“The people who have gone through things, you know, we’re supposed to shrink. I don’t do that. That is not me. I’m bold.”
Lilian Raven

At the center of Lilian’s work is the belief that people should never feel pressured to make themselves smaller because of what they have survived.

Through fashion, teaching, and art, Lilian encourages people to express themselves fully rather than hiding from their experiences.

TodayCreating space

Today, Lilian continues to build a life rooted in fashion, teaching, and community. Through her work, she has transformed survival into creativity, using art and self expression to help others feel visible, empowered, & understood.

What once began beside her mother’s sewing machine has grown into a life dedicated to healing, connection, and creating space for people to fully be themselves.
