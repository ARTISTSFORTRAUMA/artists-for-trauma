AFT & Laura Sharpe's Artists Profile

Onofre Banderas

Turning Trauma Into Purpose Through Art

Photo/Design Per BernalInterview Tattiahna WelshJuly 2026

Artist, Trauma Survivor, Father and Community Leader

After surviving a shooting with a 12 gauge shotgun, Onofre Banderas, Ono, turned trauma into purpose through art.

“Sometimes you just gotta go through the pain & suffering. That is the only way you get to really appreciate life.”
Onofre Banderas

Today, Ono uses creativity to build community and help others heal. He credits his mother’s decision to enroll him in Kids Arts at just four years old with inspiring him to become an artist and laying the foundation for the life he currently leads.

Kids ArtsKids Arts became his escape

Through drawing, painting, theater, field trips, and time spent with mentors and other children, he found a place where he could simply be a kid.

The free Saturday program served orphaned children, kids from broken families, and those raised by single parents, giving them a safe space to create, connect, and belong.

As Ono got older, life grew increasingly difficult. His dad wasn’t in the picture for much, and after losing his older brother to cancer, his mother was left raising the family on her own.

There were times they lived in group homes and hotels as she worked to provide for her children.

The compassion others showed him during those years inspired the work he now does through Pura Avenida, and Artists for Trauma, helping others experience the same sense of hope and belonging he once received.

“Programs like Kids Arts are very important for children to experience, because it could really distract them from what’s going on at home, inspire them, and help them discover a passion for art.”
Onofre Banderas

2019Ono’s life changed in an instant

An ordinary stop at a local taco stand became a life changing tragedy. While waiting in line, Ono became the victim of an assault with a deadly weapon.

He was shot twice with a 12-gauge shotgun, once in the stomach and once in his left hand.

He immediately collapsed to the ground, bleeding out. Reflecting on that moment, Ono says;

“My whole life just flashed right past me, like it literally is a movie.”
Onofre Banderas

He spent months in the hospital fighting for his life, underwent countless surgeries, and woke from a coma facing a future filled with uncertainty. In an instant, the life he had always known was gone.

Ono shares that recovery became just as much a mental battle as a physical one.

When Ono woke up from his coma, he couldn’t walk. He had lost the use and sensitivity of his left hand and was learning to live with a colostomy bag.

The independence he had always known had been stripped away, and the life he had built as a father and provider suddenly looked completely different.

But he refused to let trauma define him. Providing for his children gave him something to fight for. Forced to reinvent the life he had once known, Ono chose to keep moving forward, one day at a time.

“Your willingness to want to live is extremely, extremely important.”
Onofre Banderas

For Ono, healing was not about returning to the person he had been. It was about choosing to keep living for the people who needed him most.

In the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic, Ono was isolated in a hospital bed, separated from his family and uncertain what life would look like after recovery.

During that time, trauma surgeon and Artists for Trauma Board Member, Doctor Thomas Duncan, introduced Ono to Laura Sharpe, founder of Artists for Trauma.

Laura visited Ono in the hospital with art supplies, giving him a creative outlet and a reminder that he wasn’t alone.

“Laura showed me there are still righteous people willing to show love and support to someone they don’t even know.”
Onofre Banderas

That simple act of kindness became a turning point.

Laura and Artists for Trauma restored Ono’s hope and reconnected him with creativity during one of the darkest chapters of his life, he became determined to one day pay that same compassion forward.

RehabilitationRebuilding strength

Art became a central part of Ono’s rehabilitation, helping him heal both physically and emotionally.

In recent years, he has begun taking ceramics to rebuild strength and sensitivity in his left hand.

What started as a way to improve mobility has expanded into sculpture, murals, photography, and mixed media, allowing him to continue exploring new creative outlets.

During his recovery, Ono promised himself that if he survived, he would spend the rest of his life giving back to the people and communities that had uplifted him. Through Pura Avenida and Artists for Trauma, he found ways to put that promise into action.

“I told myself the day that I get out of the hospital… I’m going to give back in tremendous ways.”
Onofre Banderas

Today, Ono volunteers using creativity to support others navigating trauma just as it supported him during his own recovery.

CreativityMore than self expression

Ono’s life-altering traumatic experience transformed the way he views creativity.

For Ono, art is more than self expression. It creates a meditative space where people can slow down, process difficult emotions, reconnect with themselves, and begin to heal.

Through Artists for Trauma and his own recovery, Ono has witnessed creativity help veterans, trauma survivors, children, caregivers, first responders, and hospital staff alike.

To Ono, creativity isn’t just therapeutic. It belongs in every healing journey.

Pura AvenidaA 600 foot public artwork

That philosophy now shapes the work Ono does through Pura Avenida.

A community driven arts organization that creates public art, celebrates culture, brings artists and neighborhoods together through creativity.

One of the organization’s largest projects, united more than sixty artists and local children to create Ventura County’s largest community mural, a 600 foot public artwork.

The goal wasn’t simply to paint a wall. It was to build community, give local artists and neighborhood families the opportunity to create something together, and inspire pride in the place they call home. By inviting children and residents to leave their mark on the mural, the project became a lasting reminder that they, too, are part of their community’s story.

TodayPaying it forward

While pursuing a degree in art, Ono continues to volunteer at Artists for Trauma’s workshops, including the Rollettes Experience, and mentor young artists, always striving to grow as an artist so he can better serve others through creativity.

The opportunities Kids Arts gave him as a child, and the hope Artists for Trauma gave him during recovery, continue to guide everything he does.

“What Kids Arts gave me, now I’m able to pay that forward.”
Onofre Banderas

Ono’s vision extends far beyond creating art.

One day he hopes to open a community art center where children, adults, veterans, & trauma survivors can access free art supplies, creative workshops, and a safe place to heal. He imagines artists, educators, and mental health professionals working together to make creativity accessible to everyone.

From the support he received through Kids Arts, to the hope he found through Laura Sharpe and AFT, to the communities he now serves through Pura Avenida, every chapter of Ono’s story reflects the same belief: creativity has the power to bring people together, restore hope, and help people heal.
