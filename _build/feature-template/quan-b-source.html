R&B artist, gospel singer and former athlete Quan.B is building a sound shaped by faith, family & the music that raised him.

Rhythm & Roots

Design & Photo Per BernalInterview Tattiahna Welsh

Music runs deep in Quan.B’s family. Raised on Louisville’s West End, Quan grew up surrounded by gospel, faith and a deeply musical family.

Church was where he developed his voice; home was where music became something shared between fathers, sons, brothers and sisters.

Three albums into a solo R&B career, while still performing gospel with his brothers in Sonz of Zion, Quan is building from everything that came before.

His fatherFrom the stage into the aisle

Much of that foundation came from his father, Nathaniel Brown, a minister, gospel quartet singer, and bass guitarist.

Quan remembers watching him perform in church, sometimes singing his way from the stage into the aisle as the congregation clapped along.

Quan’s father passed away recently, but his presence remains vivid.

“That’s what I always looked forward to, being that front guy, being in front of people and giving them that energy.”
Quan.B

FamilyThirteen siblings, one sound

Quan is one of 13 siblings between both sides of his family, and they all sing or play instruments.

Quan plays piano, drums, bass guitar and violin. Except for violin, he says the instruments were largely self-taught.

His brothers grew up singing alongside him, harmonizing at bus stops and inside stores. When their father wouldn’t let them join his quartet, they formed their own: Sonz of Zion, now together for roughly a decade. His sisters formed the gospel quartet Faithful Connection.

Quan says his father was Bobby Brown’s second cousin, another branch of a family tree already crowded with musicians.

Church“I Told the Storm”

But church was where Quan’s own voice took shape. One of his earliest solos was “I Told the Storm.”

Growing up primarily with his mother on Louisville’s West End, Quan remembers periods without running water or electricity as she cared for six children.

The song spoke to what he had already lived through. Music became more than something to perform. It became somewhere to put those experiences.

FootballAnother love

Music shared space with another love: football. Quan entered the sport through the Big Brothers program, when his Big Brother signed him up as a child.

He played through high school, drew interest from college scouts, and says he committed to Oregon.

Two weeks later, a collision left him unconscious with what he describes as a level-four concussion and temporarily paralyzed for approximately four minutes. He was told he could never play again. Depression followed.

“Music is my outlet. It’s where I vent, where I can talk about my problems and my issues. It feels good to release them.”
Quan.B

R&BAnother lane

When pursuing football at the next level was no longer an option, music gave him a way forward. Gospel remained part of Quan’s identity, but R&B opened another lane.

His first album arrived in 2021, when Quan says he was 23, after he began collaborating with longtime friend and producer Adam Gibson, whom he had known since middle school.

The partnership started with “Let’s Talk About It.” One song led to another, eventually growing into Quan’s first album and the beginning of a solo career.

“Let’s Talk About It” showed Quan how far music could reach. Men began reaching out to tell him the song had helped them through difficult moments.

Particularly when men struggled to express themselves or had been made to feel that their emotions didn’t matter.

“I felt like I got to be a voice for a lot of men with that song.”
Quan.B

The musicThree albums, a fourth on the way

Quan realized that something created as an outlet for himself could become an outlet for other men carrying the same emotions.

Quan’s albums trace that evolution. He describes his first album Long Story Short as telling his story in condensed form. Comprehension, album number two, as an invitation to understand Quan.B, and Identification, album number three, as a deeper exploration of his identity and childhood.

A fourth album, Pressure and Patience, is on the way.

Quan is also a father of two. His son has begun singing alongside him and Sonz of Zion, carrying the family’s musical tradition into another generation.

LFA 235Back into the arena

Then music brought athletics back into the picture.

Through his work at Blueprint 502, which he describes as helping young people earn diplomas or GEDs while connecting them with jobs and housing, Quan crossed paths with Eddie Donaldson of TRUST Agency.

Eddie needed someone to sing the national anthem at LFA 235 in Louisville, Kentucky, where Artists for Trauma & Athletes for Trauma were sponsors. Quan happened to walk into the room during the conversation and landed this opportunity.

“Stepping into the octagon was different. All eyes were on me, the lights were shining, the announcer was announcing me. It was a big moment because I had never experienced that as Quan.B.”
Quan.B

Artists for Trauma“Like big sister, little brother”

Soon, the former football player was standing inside an LFA octagon with a microphone in his hand.

Years after an injury closed the door on the athletic future Quan had imagined, music had brought him back into an arena.

LFA also deepened his relationship with Eddie, whom Quan calls a “Big Bro.” Through Eddie and TRUST Agency, Quan later connected with Laura Sharpe, founder of Artists for Trauma & Athletes for Trauma.

Quan describes his first meeting with Laura “like big sister, little brother connecting,” an immediate bond that quickly moved into a heart-to-heart conversation.

Quan’s connection to the mission reaches directly into his own experience. He knows what it means to have an athletic path changed by serious injury, to experience depression afterward, and to find another direction through creativity.

It is what Quan recognizes in the people Artists for Trauma & Athletes for Trauma support:

Lives altered by trauma, but not defined by it.

“I think what Laura & Eddie are doing is changing lives, giving people the encouragement and inspiration to keep going.”
Quan.B

Full circleRhythm & roots

The mission speaks to a journey Quan understands well: losing one version of the future and finding the strength to build another.

Gospel and R&B. Football and music. Athlete and artist. Quan.B’s life has a way of circling back to the same place.

He describes his musical foundation as a root planted in childhood, something that grows whenever he sings. It reaches back to Louisville’s West End, his father moving from the church stage into the aisle, brothers harmonizing at bus stops and a young Quan discovering how much a song could carry.

And with every song, Quan carries those roots forward, turning everything that shaped him into something distinctly his own.
