About

Creativity. Connectivity. Community. – It takes a Village!

AFT offers fun, adaptive art programs, free of charge and evidence-based, that support the mental, physical and emotional well-being of trauma survivors.

About Artists For Trauma

Injury is the leading cause of death for Americans ages 1 to 44. Each year, injuries lead to more than 43 million emergency department visits and over 270,000 deaths.

Trauma survivor Laura Sharpe founded AFT in 2011, inspired by her own recovery, in which art became a source of healing from near-fatal injuries.

AFT shares the blueprint of that recovery to help other Level 1 trauma survivors reconnect with themselves, their families and their communities. Through artistic expression and human connection, we help survivors process complex emotions, rebuild confidence and find self-acceptance, and speed their recovery.

Trauma takes many forms: a car crash, a fire, a plane crash, an accident on the job, a gunshot, an explosion, a catastrophic fall, war, rape, emotional and physical violence, racism and discrimination. Some survivors don’t remember the moment of impact. Some remember only waking up in the hospital, their lives changed for good. Disfigurement, disability and a long, difficult recovery often follow.

What is the challenge of surviving this level of trauma?

Re-creating a new version of one’s life… physically, mentally and emotionally. Artists for Trauma gives survivors a platform for “creative engagement,” a proven pathway to reconnect with themselves and their community.

Awareness lets us see the possibilities within ourselves and the world; creativity lets us act on them. AFT strengthens both by pairing each survivor with an artist whose discipline speaks to their heart.

All Artists for Trauma workshops are FREE of charge to veterans and civilian trauma survivors.

Some causes of disabling and/or disfiguring trauma statistics:

Spinal Cord Injuries in the United States

  • About 311,000 Americans live with a traumatic spinal cord injury.
  • About 18,500 new injuries occur each year.
  • 78% of new injuries happen to men; the average age at injury is 44.
  • About 60% of injuries result in tetraplegia and 40% in paraplegia.
  • Leading causes: vehicle crashes (37%), falls (33%), violence (15%) and sports (8%).

Source: National Spinal Cord Injury Statistical Center, Facts and Figures at a Glance, 2026

Traumatic Brain Injuries (TBI) in the United States

  • About 214,000 TBI-related hospitalizations (2020) and 68,663 TBI-related deaths (2023): more than 586 hospitalizations and 190 deaths every day.
  • Falls are the leading cause of TBI-related hospitalizations.
  • Explosive blasts are the most frequent cause of TBI among deployed U.S. service members.

Sources: CDC, TBI Data; CDC TBI Surveillance Report; Journal of Neurotrauma

Burns

  • Globally, burns cause an estimated 180,000 deaths every year, most in low- and middle-income countries.
  • In the U.S., about 398,000 fire- or burn-related injuries are treated each year, and about 29,000 people are hospitalized for burns.
  • Non-fatal burn injuries are a leading cause of long-term illness and disability.

Sources: World Health Organization; American Burn Association

Injury figures: CDC, FastStats: All Injuries and CDC WISQARS.
