AFT & Laura Sharpe's Artists Profile

Ford Austin

The first student artist.
From trauma to creative healing.

Cover photo & design Per BernalInterview & story Tattiahna Welsh

Six months after surviving a catastrophic car accident, Ford Austin finally returned home from Cedars-Sinai Medical Center in Los Angeles.

The lifesaving surgeries were behind Ford, but the road ahead remained uncertain. Recovery no longer revolved around operating rooms and intensive care units. Instead, it unfolded quietly at home, with each day bringing him new physical, emotional and mental challenges.

Beside him, through every moment, was his wife, Lauree Dash Austin. After months of hospitalization and rehabilitation, she had become far more than a spouse. She was Ford's advocate, researcher, organizer and constant source of strength.

Although neither of them knew it yet, another trauma survivor would soon enter their lives and forever change Ford's recovery, leading him to become the first student artist of Artists for Trauma.

“No one can hold something this epic alone.”
Lauree Dash Austin

BeforeA life built on storytelling

Long before trauma became part of their story, Ford and Lauree had built lives rooted in creativity and storytelling. Ford spent decades pursuing his passion as an actor before expanding into writing, directing and producing independent films.

Lauree built an Emmy Award-winning career producing television that educated, inspired and connected audiences through meaningful stories.

Creativity wasn't simply their profession. It's how they experienced life together and how they strengthened the partnership they shared. After years of building successful careers, they looked forward to the future with excitement. Neither could have imagined that their greatest challenge would become the story that inspires countless others.

March 9, 2011Everything changed

Everything changed in the early morning hours of March 9, 2011. Driving home after a late night at the studio in LA, Ford was involved in a devastating car accident that left him fighting for his life.

He suffered a traumatic brain injury, a shattered pelvis, broken hip and femur, collapsed lungs, a ruptured spleen, kidney failure, extensive internal injuries and cardiac arrest. The trauma was so severe doctors estimated Ford had only a one percent chance of surviving.

1%Doctors' estimate of his chance of survival
10 daysIn a medically induced coma
6 monthsIn hospital and rehabilitation
15 yearsSince the night that changed everything

In a matter of moments, the life they spent years building was replaced by a fight to keep Ford alive. Teams of trauma surgeons and specialists worked around the clock to stabilize his injuries. Ford underwent surgery after surgery and was kept in a medically induced coma for 10 days, while Lauree faced the risk of losing the person she loved most.

Lauree stepped into a role she never expected herself to fill. Each morning she attended trauma rounds, took detailed notes, researched procedures, sought second opinions and built relationships with the physicians, nurses and specialists guiding Ford's care.

She was part of the team responsible for his recovery, and she was determined to understand every decision made on Ford's behalf.

Lauree came to embrace a lesson that continues to shape the way she views caregiving: “Nobody can hold something this epic alone.”

Today, Ford and Lauree continue to speak with profound gratitude about the extraordinary team of trauma surgeons, physicians, nurses, rehab specialists, therapists, family members and friends who never stopped believing in Ford's recovery, and helped carry both of them through those dark weeks and months.

Coming homeSurvival and healing were not the same

When returning home after six months of hospitalization, Ford faced paralysis on the left side of his body, damaged vocal cords from intubation, and the daunting task of relearning many of life's simplest tasks. Recovery came through many small victories, slowly restoring his independence.

Medicine saved his life, but survival and true healing were not the same. His body was recovering. Rebuilding confidence, identity and hope, however, required what no prescription could provide by itself.

Doctor Andrea Feinberg saw that need and introduced the couple to Laura Sharpe, a fellow trauma survivor whose journey inspired a different perspective on recovery. Laura had lived experience of catastrophic trauma and could offer hope no clinical expertise could provide alone.

When entering the home of Ford and Lauree, Laura didn't ask about surgeries, medications or rehabilitation. Instead, she smiled and introduced herself with a simple sentence that immediately put Ford at ease.

“Hi, my name is Laura Sharpe. I'm like you!”
Laura Sharpe, introducing herself to Ford

Laura came as a fellow traveler on a journey that Ford was just beginning. He later described her arrival as bringing “a beautiful white light” into one of the darkest chapters of his recovery.

A new kind of healingThe opportunity to thrive

As the trio spent more time together, Laura shared a philosophy that had emerged through her own recovery. She believed healing extended beyond the physical body, and that creativity could help survivors rediscover confidence, rebuild identity and reconnect with a sense of purpose that trauma often steals away.

Laura envisioned a non-profit organization where art, music, storytelling and human connection would become important parts of the healing process. She believed that every trauma survivor deserved not only the opportunity to recover, but also the opportunity to thrive.

Ford and Lauree began to see recovery not simply as returning to the life they had before, but as discovering what life could become after trauma. One day, Laura shared her vision with Ford in a way neither of them would ever forget.

“I want to start an organization called Artists for Trauma, and I want you to be the first student artist. I want to help you.”
Laura Sharpe to Ford. He embraced her vision without hesitation.

Creativity as recoveryWalk, talk and sing

As Laura continued navigating her own recovery, the two supported each other equally through wins and losses, and healing strengthened through friendship as much as therapy. What began as a conversation was the beginning of Ford's lifelong connection with Laura and Artists for Trauma, and a friendship that continues to this day.

The two explored creative ideas that complemented traditional rehabilitation and discovered that creativity wasn't separate from recovery, but a part of the recovery itself.

Virtual skiing and tennis on a Nintendo Wii helped Ford rebuild balance and coordination, transforming physical therapy into something engaging and fun. After weeks of intubation damaged his vocal cords, Laura connected Ford with singing lessons that gradually helped restore the voice Ford feared he had lost forever.

Those experiences restored confidence, rebuilt identity and reminded Ford that even after a near-fatal trauma, there was still room for joy, creativity and growth. Looking back, Ford credits AFT with helping him reclaim pieces of himself that once seemed lost.

“AFT helped me to once again walk, talk and sing.”
Ford Austin

As the organization grew, his recovery reflected its mission, demonstrating how creativity and community can complement medical care.

Ford became much more than AFT's first student artist. A physician's referral evolved into a lifelong friendship, uniting Ford, Lauree and Laura by a shared commitment to helping others navigate life after trauma. For Ford and Lauree, the organization became an extension of the physicians, nurses, therapists, rehab specialists, family and friends whose unwavering support made Ford's recovery possible.

TodayFull circle

Ford continues to act, write, direct and produce while mentoring fellow trauma survivors. He has completed multiple 5K races and competes in croquet as well.

Inspired by her caregiving experience, Lauree completed a 400-hour meditation teacher training and advanced studies in mindfulness, Yoga Nidra, Mindful Self-Compassion and contemplative practice. She now helps others navigate trauma, grief and life's most difficult transitions.

Ford's journey has come full circle. Today, he serves on the board of Artists for Trauma, continuing the mission that began when Laura Sharpe invited him to become AFT's first student artist. With his own experience of trauma, Ford believes one of the greatest ways to honor his recovery is by giving back and helping others find hope.

For Lauree, AFT has become, in her own words, “part of the fabric of my life.” She, Ford and Laura Sharpe continue “walking the path of our lives together,” supporting trauma survivors and reminding them that healing is never a journey taken alone.

Fifteen years after their lives changed forever, their friendship continues to inspire the mission of Artists for Trauma and the countless trauma survivors, thrivers and difference makers whose lives it touches.

“I don't believe there's ever a point of no return. Every day I get better.”
Ford Austin
