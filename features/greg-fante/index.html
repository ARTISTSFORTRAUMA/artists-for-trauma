“When we win, our community wins.”

The Coach of Louisville

Interview & Story Tattiahna WelshPhotography Sarah Ann CohenDesign Per Bernal

Sports have always meant more to Greg Fante than what happens in competition. He has built his career around something bigger:

Creating the team, the opportunities, and the partnerships that allow Louisville to win.

As President and CEO of the Louisville Sports Commission, Greg is helping shape what sports can do for a city, on the field and far beyond it.

Fourth-generation LouisvillianA family story

Louisville is more than the city where Greg built his career. It is part of his family story.

A fourth-generation Louisvillian whose children now represent a fifth, he began his career in hospitality in 1984, working with sports organizations at a Louisville hotel before sports tourism had become an established industry.

Greg rose to regional general manager of three hotel properties, but sports remained a constant. By 1999, that experience placed him alongside the early development of what would become the Louisville Sports Commission.

What began as a small operation would eventually help reshape Louisville’s identity as an international sports destination.

Greg initially worked on the tourism side, identifying sporting events Louisville could attract, before officially joining the Louisville Sports Commission in 2007. After serving under each of its first three presidents, he became President and CEO in 2023.

As the organization grew, so did Greg’s philosophy on leadership.

“I see myself more as a coach than I do as an athlete… when we win, our community wins.”
Greg Fante

The impactLouisville’s number one market

Those wins can now be measured across the entire city. In 1999, sporting events generated roughly 15,000 hotel room nights annually for Louisville.

Last year, that number grew to approximately 303,000 hotel room nights in new business booked for the upcoming years, making sports Louisville’s number one market.

Greg sees the impact on people. Visitors support hotels, restaurants, and attractions, creating business for everyone from bell staff and laundry workers to restaurant servers. The impact reaches far beyond the event itself, strengthening Louisville’s local economy.

CollaborationBuilt together

Greg notes this impact requires collaboration across public agencies, private businesses, nonprofits and community organizations.

The Norton Healthcare Sports & Learning Center, developed in West Louisville alongside the Louisville Urban League and other partners, opened the city to competitions it previously could not host.

The Louisville Sports Commission helped raise funds as well as oversee improvements to the BMX track at E.P. “Tom” Sawyer State Park.

Louisville ActiveAccess to sports

As Louisville’s sports infrastructure grew, so did the mission. Bringing events into the city mattered, but so did making sure more people within the city could participate.

That mission evolved as the Louisville Sports Commission looked beyond hosting events to expanding access to sports within the community. Through Louisville Active, programs including Kids Finish First, Playing It Forward, Sports Fun(d) and We Play work to reduce barriers and create greater access to sports.

Greg remembers one young participant from Louisville’s West End who returned to Kids Finish First year after year. Through the program, he developed a genuine passion for running, progressing to 5Ks and eventually carrying that passion into athletic competition in high school.

The young man’s story reflects something that runs throughout Greg’s work:

Sometimes opportunity begins simply by giving someone a place to start.

FamilyThe Fante brothers

That connection to Louisville runs through the Fante family. Greg’s older brother, Leo, has built his career in the coffee industry and opened Fante’s Coffee in Louisville, while his younger brother, Stephen, serves as an international brand ambassador for Yellowstone Bourbon.

Greg jokes that the Fante brothers can take care of an entire day in Louisville: Leo can get you coffee in the morning, Greg can get you into a sporting event, and Stephen can finish the night with bourbon.

Greg’s early meetings with Eddie Donaldson, founder of TRUST Agency, at Fante’s Coffee eventually introduced Donaldson to Leo, and the two developed a friendship of their own.

Leo has since supported Eddie Donaldson’s events, creating “Knockout Coffee,” which he packages with an MMA theme. He also supports a fighter or two at the events.

TRUST Agency & LFACombat sports momentum

Louisville’s combat sports community was gaining momentum when Greg first connected with Eddie through mutual friend Marty Storch. Eddie’s relationship with LFA founder Ed Soares helped bring LFA to Louisville for three successful events.

Greg believes that momentum helped pave the way for UFC’s return. On June 10, 2024, UFC Fight Night: Cannonier vs. Imavov drew 19,578 fans to the KFC Yum! Center, setting a UFC Fight Night attendance record.

Artists for TraumaAlongside TRUST

Laura Sharpe’s and Artists for Trauma’s connection to Louisville’s combat sports community began well before the development of Athletes for Trauma.

AFT previously sponsored Louisville fighter Eden Hall, and Laura visited RFG Integrative Fitness alongside Lisa Mills, VP of Marketing and PR for the Louisville Sports Commission.

Laura and AFT have since been alongside Eddie throughout the growth of TRUST. Greg admires how Laura has overcome so much physically, mentally and emotionally while continuing to bring positive energy and collaborative activation to the work.

The name TRUST resonates with Greg. In an industry of driven personalities protective of their brands, he sees Eddie as a bridge between them.

“Trust Eddie and trust each other that all boats will rise if we work together.”
Greg Fante

Louisville vs. IrelandBridges Beyond Boxing

That growing network continues to bring Louisville’s combat sports community together. Most recently, the Louisville Sports Commission helped produce Bridges Beyond Boxing over Labor Day weekend, bringing young boxers from Northern Ireland and the Louisville region together for competition and cultural exchange.

The initiative came to the Louisville Sports Commission through Mayor Craig Greenberg’s office, with John Ramsey, Director of City Partnerships, driving the opportunity to the Commission. They then brought together half a dozen local sponsors, TKO Gym owner James Dixon, Eddie Donaldson, and Laura Sharpe to help produce the exchange.

Twelve young boxers from Northern Ireland joined 12 regional U.S. boxers for an experience that extended beyond the ring. The visiting athletes explored Louisville landmarks including the Muhammad Ali Center, Louisville Slugger Museum & Factory, Cave Hill Cemetery and Lakeside Swim Club, while spending five nights with members of Louisville’s Irish immigrant community who welcomed them into their homes.

October 10, 2026ONI Fights 5: MMA

That momentum now carries into ONI Fights 5: MMA, coming to the historic Galt House Hotel on October 10, 2026. The event will be streamed live on LFA platforms, bringing professional MMA into one of Louisville’s landmark hotels and reaching audiences beyond the city.

“It’s going to have a great feel, it’s going to have a great look, and more importantly, it’s going to have some great fights.”
Greg Fante

In a city shaped by combat sports legends including Muhammad Ali, Jimmy Ellis and Greg Page, ONI Fights 5: MMA builds on Louisville’s history while creating another platform for Louisville’s next chapter.

2025–2026A challenge of his own

While Greg Fante continued his work with the Louisville Sports Commission, he was navigating a major challenge of his own.

In 2025, after being diagnosed with prostate cancer, Greg underwent 25 rounds of radiation treatment. He entered 2026 believing that chapter was behind him and returned to work, but soon noticed his energy beginning to disappear.

Following an early morning Sports Commission event at Churchill Downs during Derby week, Greg went to a medical appointment. Diagnostic testing revealed acute myeloid leukemia (AML).

Greg began chemotherapy that same afternoon, beginning four intensive rounds of treatment and three bone marrow biopsies.

After years spent building teams, he found himself relying on the strength of his own. Greg’s team showed up and his leadership group kept the Sports Commission moving, while family, friends, colleagues and partners surrounded him with support. Meals arrived. Flowers were sent. People helped around his home, and relationships built across decades reached back to him when he needed them most.

“I cried more tears of happiness and just respect for others than I ever did about the situation that I was in.”
Greg Fante

Greg called the experience an “accounting of life,” changing his perspective on the relationships and work that mattered most.

Gymnastics City USALouisville’s next chapter

Greg returned to his work with Louisville’s next chapter already taking shape.

In 2028, the city will become Gymnastics City USA, hosting fifteen days of events culminating in the U.S. Olympic Team Trials: Gymnastics at the KFC Yum! Center. Months later, the Solheim Cup will arrive at Valhalla Golf Club.

Greg’s definition of success reaches beyond the next major event. It lives in the young athlete given a chance to participate, the worker who benefits when visitors come to town, the organizations that accomplish more together and a community brought together by sports.

After a career built around teams, and a year that showed him the strength of his own, Greg’s vision for Louisville remains rooted in the same belief:

“The more success that we have as a team and a community, the better our city is going to be.”
Greg Fante
