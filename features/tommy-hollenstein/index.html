From a young age, Tommy’s life was shaped by two forces: art and adrenaline.

Creating with his hands while constantly chasing movement. BMX. Surfing. Mountain biking.

“I just love adrenaline. I love excitement.”
Tommy Hollenstein

BeforeArt & adrenaline

A life built on movement. On risk. On pushing limits.

Then everything changed…

A bike crash and spinal cord injury redefined Tommy’s life in an instant.

FaithA second chance

For over 41 years, Tommy has lived as a quadriplegic. He describes looking down at his body and asking God for a second chance. That faith carried him through and continues to guide him forward.

While healing and rehabilitating, he began mouth stick painting. But it did not feel fluid or natural.

Coming from a background in extreme sports, he was used to movement, motion and freedom. That instinct led Tommy to something new: incorporating his wheelchair into the process itself.

“I paint with the tires on my wheelchair. It’s kind of a different way to paint, but it works well for me.”
Tommy Hollenstein

Art in motionEverything clicked

The need for movement didn’t stop after the accident. It just changed, and he went on to find new creative ways to move, adapt and keep going.

That same need for movement found its way onto the canvas. Rolling his wheelchair through paint alongside his service dog, everything clicked…

From a bicycle accident to the tires of his chair, at this moment everything came full circle.

When he paints, it feels like a dance, like gliding, like surfing a wave. Building each piece, layer by layer, over time.

“I turned something most people would consider being a negative, my wheelchair, into a positive outlet for my freedom and my expression.”
Tommy Hollenstein

Artists for TraumaGiving back

Today, Tommy creates from his home studio and also works out of Risk Rock Studios.

Through his work with Artists for Trauma and his connection with founder Laura Sharpe, he extends that same outlet to others. Using art to give back and create space for healing, connection and possibility.

Through AFT, organizations like Canine Companions and Shane’s Inspiration, Tommy continues to give back. Using his art to raise money and support causes that change lives immediately.

Art gave him his freedom back, guided by faith. Now, Tommy uses it to help others find theirs.
